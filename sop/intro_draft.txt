My research interests center on reinforcement learning for post-training foundation models. A recent shift has shown how powerful it can be: instead of learning only from human-written examples, models can now be trained with rewards that check whether their answers are correct, and through this process they learn to reason step by step. As training continues, they even learn on their own to think longer before responding, which opens a second way to improve them: spending more computation at test time. Yet this approach works best where answers can be checked automatically, such as math and code. In many other settings, models must rely on learned reward models, which judge outputs less reliably and can be exploited during training. In my PhD, I hope to contribute to this area by studying how learned reward models can give more reliable, step-level feedback, both for training models with reinforcement learning and for selecting among their outputs at test time.
